## 1. Primary result: utility-weighted vs equal-weight climate gradients

The two models compared:
- **D (proposed):** the two-branch NB model with target-relative lags,
  where the climate encoder's gradient is weighted by per-fold measured
  shuffle utilities.
- **B (baseline):** the identical model with equal weights.

Everything else in the two runs is identical. Metric: headline MAE (folds
1,2,3,6,7,8,9), 5 seeds, seed-mean estimand
(`tables/primary_D_vs_B.csv`).

| h | D − B | Holm p (t) | Wilcoxon p | D better in | 95% CI (block bootstrap) | Frozen verdict |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| 1 | +0.063 | 0.78 | 0.81 | 3/7 folds | [−0.030, +0.174] | non-inferior (margin 0.30) |
| 2 | +0.082 | 0.60 | 0.38 | 3/7 | [−0.032, +0.213] | non-inferior (margin 0.30) |
| 3 | +0.068 | 0.78 | 0.47 | 3/7 | [−0.071, +0.226] | no measurable difference |
| 4 | +0.049 | 0.78 | 0.69 | 3/7 | [−0.105, +0.205] | no measurable difference |

**Finding: weighting the climate encoder's gradient by measured
per-horizon utility did not improve forecasts.** The point estimates are
slightly worse at every horizon, but no difference is distinguishable
from zero. The result holds across estimands and fold sets
(`tables/primary_bootstrap_estimands.csv`):
- seed ensemble: +0.05 to +0.06;
- all 9 folds: +0.05 to +0.07.

Every CI includes zero.

**How the uncertainty was computed**
- Paired 4-week blocks of contiguous forecast origins.
- All 25 districts in a block are resampled together, and blocks are
  resampled within each fold.
- Seeds are averaged per cell first, so repeated seeds are not treated as
  independent.
- Horizons are analysed separately, because one origin's four targets
  overlap.
- Folds share expanding training histories, so the fold-level t and
  Wilcoxon tests (7 folds) are optimistic. The block bootstrap is the more
  conservative summary.

Figure: `figures/primary_per_fold_differences.png` shows the per-fold D − B
and the headline mean with its CI.

## 4. Architecture and utility controls (exploratory; 3 seeds; not multiplicity-adjusted)

Headline MAE difference from B, negative = better (`tables/controls_vs_B.csv`):

| Arm | h1 | h2 | h3 | h4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A origin-relative lags, equal weights | +0.20 | +0.30 | +0.43 | +0.51 |
| **C origin-relative lags, measured weights** | **−0.44** | **−0.40** | **−0.33** | **−0.27** |
| D target-relative, measured (proposed; 5 seeds) | +0.06 | +0.08 | +0.07 | +0.05 |
| E fixed weights 1,2,3,4 | −0.11 | −0.09 | −0.10 | −0.11 |
| F climate gradients from h=4 only | −0.00 | +0.53 | +1.17 | +0.26 |
| G case-only-utility weights | +0.46 | +0.54 | +0.64 | +0.72 |
| H GradNorm restricted to the climate group | −0.04 | +0.02 | +0.03 | +0.04 |

- **Lag alignment interacts with weighting.**
  - With equal weights, target-relative lags beat origin-relative (A is
    worse than B).
  - With measured weights, origin-relative lags are the best arm overall
    (C − D = −0.50 / −0.48 / −0.40 / −0.32; Wilcoxon p 0.016 / 0.016 /
    0.047 / 0.078).
  - C was **not** a declared configuration. It has 3 seeds and is
    exploratory, so it needs confirmation on new data before any claim.
- **Other controls.**
  - Fixed [1,2,3,4] is slightly better than B (5/7 folds, p ≥ 0.11).
  - GradNorm is indistinguishable from B.
  - Restricting climate learning to h=4 leaves the h=1–3 climate heads at
    zero and hurts h=2–3.
  - Case-only-utility weights hurt.

**GradNorm** is from Chen, Badrinarayanan, Lee and Rabinovich, ICML 2018
(PMLR 80; verified). Here it is restricted to the climate group, and its
deviations are listed in the implementation doc §15.

## 5. What the weights and utilities measure

Figures: `figures/weights_vs_uniform.png` and
`figures/signed_utilities.png`. Table: `tables/weights_by_fold.csv`.

- **Shuffle reliance (primary utility).** For every fold and horizon the
  frozen equal-weight pilot got worse when its weather windows were
  swapped with same-season windows from other years. The gain rises with
  horizon in every fold (0.11–2.44 MAE). The resulting weights are far
  from uniform: max/min 1.7–4.0; for example fold 6 is
  0.46 / 0.81 / 1.18 / 1.54.
  - This measures how much a *trained* model **relies** on correctly
    aligned weather.
  - It does not measure what climate adds over a model without it, and it
    does not measure causal effect: the shuffle breaks the alignment of
    weather with time, not weather's mechanism.
- **Case-only incremental benefit (sensitivity utility).** The relative
  gain of the climate model over a size-matched case-only model is small
  and often negative (−0.03 to +0.12), with sign changes across folds and
  seeds. The resulting weights are erratic (max/min up to 77). Arm G, which
  used them, was the worst arm.
- **Causality.** None of these analyses identifies a causal climate
  effect. The strongest statement supported is predictive: in the
  frozen-model perturbation, forecasts at h ≥ 2–3 depend on weather
  aligned in time. Earlier capacity-matched shuffle ablations (short paper
  §4.8; benchmark) point the same way.

## 6. Delay kernels, gates and corrections

Figures: `figures/rainfall_delay_kernels.png` and
`figures/gates_and_corrections.png`. Tables:
`tables/rainfall_kernel_summary.csv`, `kernel_vs_scan_spearman.csv` and
`gates_and_corrections.csv`.

**Learned rainfall kernels**, as the centre of mass in target-relative
delay d = h + k (mean over districts, seeds and folds 1–9):

| Arm | h=1 | h=2 | h=3 | h=4 | Usable mass h=1→4 |
| --- | --- | --- | --- | --- | --- |
| A, origin-relative | 6.6 | 7.6 | 8.6 | 9.6 | 1 (all) |
| B, target-relative | 8.0 | 8.3 | 8.6 | 8.9 | 0.96 → 0.80 |
| C, origin-relative | 7.0 | 8.0 | 9.0 | 10.0 | 1 (all) |
| D, target-relative | 8.7 | 8.9 | 9.2 | 9.6 | 0.96 → 0.81 |

- **Target-relative kernels** place about 8–10 weeks of delay at every
  horizon. Measured weights shift D's kernels about 0.6 weeks longer
  than B's.
- **Origin-relative kernels** shift by one week per horizon by
  construction.
- **Agreement with the scan.** Learned per-district delays correlate
  weakly with the training-only cross-correlation peaks: Spearman 0.24–0.31
  for B and D, 0.14–0.19 for A and C, fold-to-fold SD 0.22–0.37.
  - The scan's own rainfall peak correlation (+0.04) is below its
    resolvability threshold (0.10).
  - This is descriptive agreement at best, not delay recovery.
- **Gates and corrections (headline test cells).**
  - The mean gate rises with horizon: B 0.43 → 0.56, D 0.44 → 0.56.
  - The gated climate correction grows from 0.13 to 0.29 (log-mean scale).
    The case correction grows from 0.22 to 0.27, so the climate correction
    is larger only at h = 4.
  - B and D differ by at most 0.02 on any of these quantities. Measured
    weighting barely changed what the model learned.

## 10. Summary of findings

1. **Null primary result.** Measured per-horizon climate-utility weighting
   did not improve on equal weighting, retrospectively or in 2026. It was
   non-inferior at h=1–2 within the 0.30 margin.
2. **Reliance is not incremental value.** The measured shuffle reliance of
   the pilots rises with horizon, but the case-only incremental utility is
   close to zero and unstable.
3. **Model behaviour.** The two-branch model learns 8–10-week
   target-relative rainfall delays and opens its climate gate at longer
   horizons, whichever weights are used.
4. **Unconfirmed lead.** Origin-relative lags combined with measured
   weights (arm C) did best. This was not declared and is not confirmed.
5. **Standing against other models.** No extension model beats the stored
   separate GRU-NB models significantly. In 2026 the stored models and the
   zero-shot outside comparator ranked higher at h=3–4.
